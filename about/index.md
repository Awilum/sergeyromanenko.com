# A life spent exploring through the code, cameras and the road.

## Sergey Romanenko

I am a software engineer and independent author of programming courses on [Stepik](https://sergeyromanenko.com/about/stepik/), with more than 17 years of commercial software development experience across application architecture, web development, automation, and testing.

Since 2022, I have been creating practical programming courses in SQL, JavaScript, Python, Go, PHP, Kotlin, and other subjects. My courses combine structured explanations, exercises, and verifiable assignments, helping learners develop practical skills and work independently with each technology.

I have contributed to commercial and open-source software projects, previously worked as a mentor at [Solvery.io](https://sergeyromanenko.com/about/solvery/) (2022–2023), and authored technical books and learning materials on SQL and Markdown. Alongside software engineering and education, I pursue photography and develop independent creative projects.

I’ve spent my life moving between disciplines, places, and ways of understanding the world. I began with computers and software, learning to take complicated systems apart and understand how they work. Over time, that curiosity expanded beyond the screen into teaching, photography, independent projects, and the experience of living and travelling across different places and cultures.

Today, I work across code, cameras, and education, while continuing to explore the world around me. I build software and practical tools, create learning resources, and photograph people, streets, and everyday life. Some of my work is technical and structured; some is personal, observational, and open-ended. What connects it is a desire to understand things firsthand, make something meaningful from what I discover, and share the result with others.

How it connects

## Different disciplines. A shared *curiosity about how* the world works.

01

### Engineering — Understanding How Things Work

My work in software has taught me to approach complicated problems with curiosity, patience, and a willingness to look beneath the surface. I work with application architecture, frontend development, APIs, testing, and independent technical projects. I continue to explore software through research and development, building practical solutions and investigating better ways to solve real problems.

02

### Education — Making Knowledge Useful

I turn technical experience into learning resources that help people understand concepts and apply them independently. Through programming courses, practical exercises, books, documentation, and mentoring, I work to make complex subjects more approachable without stripping away the details that matter.

03

### Photography — Paying Attention to People and Place

Photography gives me a way to explore the world through direct observation. I photograph people, streets, architecture, and everyday situations, with a growing focus on travel, street, portrait, and documentary photography. My personal projects explore the quieter and more complicated sides of human experience: belonging, distance, memory, and the feeling of trying to find a place in the world.

04

### Independent Creation — Building and Sharing

Alongside my client work and teaching, I develop open-source software, publish practical resources, and build small tools that solve specific problems. I value independent work that can be examined, reused, improved, and shared. These projects give me room to experiment, follow an idea beyond its initial purpose, and make something useful available to other people.

  I develop independent photographic projects focused on people, places, streets and everyday life. My professional work has covered portraiture, street photography, fashion, photojournalism and documentary photography. Earlier in this period I also organized photography walks, creative projects and workshops, bringing photographers and models together to learn from one another. My current interests include travel photography, street photography, portraits, and visual documentation that captures the atmosphere and complexity of lived experience.
2. 2023–present
  #### [Online Education](https://sergeyromanenko.com/about/stepik/)
  [Read the full chapter →](https://sergeyromanenko.com/about/stepik/)
  I create and teach programming courses on Stepik, covering subjects including SQL, JavaScript, Python, PHP, Go, Kotlin, Java, C# and Markdown. The catalog spans individual trainers and bundled career programs, and the figures behind it — courses, lessons, enrollments and learner reviews — are read from the same local dataset the course pages are built from, so they stay current without being typed in by hand.
3. present
  #### Software & Research and Development
  I continue to work with software through independent development, technical problem-solving and research and development. My background includes frontend engineering, application architecture, APIs, automation, testing, localization, and broader contract development. I remain interested in how software can solve practical problems and how existing systems can be improved.
4. 2010–present
  #### Open-Source Software
  I develop and contribute to open-source software across PHP, JavaScript, frontend tooling, content-management systems and developer utilities. In 2010 I created Monstra, a flat-file CMS that received the Softpedia Pick Award and Softpedia Script of the Day, and I actively developed it, maintaining and supporting the community that grew around it. In 2018 I worked on Flextype, another CMS, which is included in the collection of content management systems featured on Jamstack.org. I also contribute to other open-source projects and take part in smaller developer communities, building reusable solutions and sharing practical tools and knowledge.
5. present
  #### Books & Publications
  I create educational publications and practical learning resources, including open materials. My publishing work focuses on making technical knowledge accessible through clear explanations, useful examples, and formats that readers can use independently.
6. present
  #### Independent Tools & Logic Games
  I build small, practical tools and logic games in response to real needs. These projects combine software development, experimentation, and a preference for useful solutions without unnecessary complexity.
