# Photography, Creative Projects & Model Scouting

From 2016 to 2019, photography was a central part of my professional and creative life. I
developed personal photography projects, founded and organized a photography community,
worked with model agencies, photographed fashion and events, and discovered new models.

The main activities, projects and tools behind this chapter.

- **Creative work:** Independent creative photography projects, portrait photography and model photoshoots
- **Photography community:** Photo Walks (Fotoprogulki), founded and organized by me; the community continued after my departure
- **Agency photography:** Sigma Models: fashion shows, castings and agency events

This page documents my active photography and model-related work from 2016 through 2019. Over these four years, I developed creative photography projects, founded and organized Photo Walks, photographed fashion shows and agency events with Sigma Models, and worked as a freelance model scout for Liberty Model Agency. The Photo Walks community continued after I stepped away; that later continuity is separate from my own active work period.

In short

From 2016 to 2019, I combined independent creative photography with collaborative photo walks, agency photography and freelance model scouting. I worked on portrait, fashion, event and commercial shoots; created opportunities for photographers and aspiring models to work together; and continually developed my photography through workshops, masterclasses and practical experience. This page brings together the projects, activities, equipment and feedback that document those four years.

03 · Developing My Photography Skills

## An apprenticeship with no classroom.

Between 2016 and 2019, I developed my photography through independent projects, practical
shoots, workshops, professional events and constant experimentation.

Across these four years, I invested considerable time and resources in becoming a better photographer. My development combined workshops and masterclasses, model photography, exhibitions, equipment research and a substantial amount of practical work. I learned by photographing people, experimenting with different approaches, studying the results and continually refining my technique.

Masterclasses and workshops

I attended masterclasses and workshops led by working photographers and other professionals from the industry, and took away technique, workflow and honest feedback.

Model photography workshops

I took part in workshops built around working with models: direction, posing, communication, and the rhythm of a productive session.

Exhibitions and equipment events

I visited photography exhibitions and events dedicated to cameras, lenses, lighting and new photography technology.

Attending and hosting photography-related events

I attended and hosted photography-related events: masterclasses, workshops, exhibitions, agency events and community gatherings.

Technique and lighting

I explored portrait photography, composition, lighting setups and studio lighting, and spent time experimenting with different approaches.

Regular practice

I practised constantly: shoots, experiments and a lot of unglamorous repetition. This was a period of active learning, not passive interest.

I will not name the photographers, exhibitions or workshops I attended: I cannot verify the names reliably from this distance, and a name I get wrong is worse than no name. What I can say precisely is what this period was made of: structured learning, model photography, equipment culture, and constant experimentation.

04 · Building a Photography Community

## Photo Walks: a community I founded and built.

Fotoprogulki, literally “Photo Walks”: an independent photography community I founded and
actively organized from 2016 to 2019 for photographers and aspiring models.

I founded Photo Walks (Fotoprogulki) to create a friendly environment where people interested in photography could meet, practise, learn from one another and make photographs together. From 2016 through 2019, I invited beginner photographers and aspiring models to collaborative photo walks and creative sessions. I organized participants, found suitable locations, coordinated sessions and schedules, and helped create an environment where people could gain practical experience.

Photo Walks was an independent photography community, not a commercial photography agency. It was built around collaboration, communication, creative experimentation and the enjoyment of photography rather than formal commercial obligations.

Thanks to Sergey for organizing photo sessions and photo walks. Thanks to him, my photographs and my skills improved.

05 · Creative Photography Projects

## Making photographs beyond the assignment.

From 2016 to 2019, I worked on independent creative photography projects and model-led
photoshoots, developing visual ideas through portraiture, experimentation and
collaboration.

Not all of my photography was created for an agency, an event or a commercial client. Alongside those assignments, I worked on self-directed creative photography projects and photoshoots with models. I developed ideas into photographs, using portraiture and collaborative sessions to explore subjects, moods and visual directions.

These projects gave me room to experiment with composition, lighting, atmosphere, styling and black-and-white imagery. They also helped me develop a more personal approach to photographing people and expanded the creative perspective I brought to my portrait, fashion and event photography.

Independent creative work was an important part of my photography between 2016 and 2019. It existed alongside my community activities, agency assignments and freelance model scouting rather than being limited to one commercial direction.

The photographs presented elsewhere on this page are selected examples from my archive, not a complete record of every creative project from those four years.

06 · Event and Fashion Photography

## Assignments, occasions and crowds.

From 2016 to 2019, I photographed fashion shows, castings and agency events connected with
Sigma Models, alongside commercial and independent photography assignments.

From 2016 to 2019, I worked with Sigma Models (Sigma Model Agency) as a photographer, covering the agency's fashion shows and providing freelance photography coverage for agency events, castings and other model-related activities.

This work taught me event photography, portraiture and fashion photography the honest way: under time constraints, in front of moving people, in loud rooms. It also gave me practice keeping my nerve when there were only minutes to find the right angle and make the photograph.

Some of the models I met and photographed during this period later became creative collaborators. I continued working with some of them on independent and artistic photoshoots beyond the original agency-related activities.

It is important to distinguish these two directions. My work with Sigma Models was photography: covering fashion shows and agency-related events. My work with Liberty Model Agency was freelance model scouting, described in the next section. These were separate professional activities, not a single full-time agency position.

Types of work I photographed

- Fashion shows and model-related events.
- Castings and agency gatherings.
- Local festivals, including cosplay and anime-related events.
- Advertising and promotional photography.
- Commercial photoshoots involving shops and local businesses.
- Individual portrait sessions and creative model photoshoots.

Documented, named examples

- **Trial Sport:** A photo shoot in the Trial Sport sports shop, mentioned in a review on this page.
- **Pelikan Kids:** A commercial photo shoot for Pelikan Kids, reviewed on this page.
- **LIK beauty salon anniversary:** Event photography at the birthday anniversary of the LIK beauty salon.
- **Gubernsky Style:** Event photography at the Gubernsky Style event, where frames were made in minutes.
- **All-Russian anime festival:** Photographing a cosband at the festival, with little time and few good spots.

The range mattered to me. Some days were planned studio sessions with time to think; other days were crowded public events where the right frame existed for a few seconds. Both taught me something the other could not.

07 · Model Scouting

## Finding faces for Liberty Model Agency.

From 2016 to 2019, I worked with Liberty Model Agency as a freelance model scout,
discovering potential models through social networks, local communities and
fashion-related events.

From 2016 to 2019, I worked with Liberty Model Agency as a freelance model scout. I participated in fashion-related events, engaged with local communities and social networks, and discovered potential new faces for the agency.

I actively used social networks, local communities and fashion-related events to discover people who might be interested in modeling. Scouting required more than simply photographing someone and forwarding their pictures. It involved recognizing photographic potential, identifying interesting appearances, communicating with people and understanding who might be suitable for modeling opportunities.

This was distinct both from the assignments I photographed with Sigma Models and from the collaborative photo walks I organized. I was not a full-time employee of either agency; these were distinct, parallel directions of work.

08 · My Camera Equipment

## Three tools, two jobs.

A Nikon D7200 and two lenses: the 85mm for portraits, the Sigma 18–35mm F1.8 for
everything wider.

The equipment described below represents the photographic kit documented in the 2018–2019 part of my archive. It is an equipment snapshot within the wider 2016–2019 story, not a claim that I used exactly the same camera and lenses throughout all four years.

### 08.1 · Nikon D7200

The Nikon D7200 was my main camera during 2018–2019. It was an important part of my photography during that period, covering portrait sessions, model photography and event work. I identify it specifically with the years for which I can substantiate its use rather than extending that claim to the entire 2016–2019 timeline.

- **Effective pixels:** 24.1 MP
- **Image sensor:** 23.5 × 15.6 mm DX-format CMOS
- **Lens mount:** Nikon F
- **Max. image resolution:** 6000 × 4000 px (DX)
- **Format:** APS-C (DX), ≈1.5× crop vs 35mm FX
- **Source:** [Nikon USA, D7100 overview ↗](https://www.nikonusa.com/p/d7100/1513/overview)

### 08.2 · Nikon 85mm

The 85mm was my main portrait lens, used primarily for portrait photography and work with models. On the DX-format D7100 it provides an angle of view comparable to approximately 127.5mm on a 35mm full-frame camera, which is a genuinely flattering distance to stand at when photographing a person: close enough for expression, far enough for comfort.

I do not state an aperture or a full product designation for it on this page, because I cannot verify the exact version from the equipment record in front of me. A wrong spec is worse than an absent one.

- **Role:** Primary portrait lens
- **Focal length:** 85 mm
- **On DX / APS-C:** ≈127.5 mm equivalent field of view
- **Use:** Portraits, working with models

### 08.3 · SIGMA 18–35mm F1.8 DC HSM | Art

The Sigma was my other important lens. I used it for wider compositions, environmental portraits, group photographs, and situations where I wanted to include more of the surroundings in the frame: the street, the studio, the venue.

- **Focal length:** 18–35 mm
- **Maximum aperture:** Constant F1.8 throughout the zoom range
- **Format:** APS-C / DX-format cameras
- **Mount:** Nikon F (version used)
- **Min. focusing distance:** 28 cm
- **Filter diameter:** 72 mm
- **Optical construction:** 17 elements in 12 groups
- **On DX / APS-C:** ≈27–52.5 mm equivalent field of view
- **Source:** [Sigma, 18–35mm F1.8 DC HSM | Art specifications ↗](https://www.sigma-global.com/en/lenses/a013_18_35_18/?tab=specification)

The practical difference was simple. The 85mm was the portrait tool, made for faces. The Sigma was the scene tool, made for everything the face lived inside. Between the two, the D7100 covered the formats documented on this page, from tight agency headshots to wide environmental frames at public events.

11 · The Next Chapter

## I left. The community continued.

A four-year chapter of photography, creative work and collaboration.

My active period of photography and model-related work ran from 2016 through 2019. During those years I developed independent creative projects, founded and organized Photo Walks, photographed fashion and agency events, and worked as a freelance model scout for Liberty Model Agency.

I eventually left the photography community because I decided to travel and move on to another chapter of my life. The community continued after my departure. What I carried forward was not just a collection of photographs, but practical experience working with people, developing creative ideas, organizing projects and building something that could continue without my day-to-day involvement.

This page documents my personal professional and creative work from 2016 to 2019. The broader history of Photo Walks and the related photography initiative is documented separately in the Photo Walk and Model Agencies article.
