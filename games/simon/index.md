# Simon

Watch the colored pads light up in a growing sequence, then repeat it exactly. Each round adds one more step. One wrong tap ends the game. Sounds, mute, and local best scores.

- **Pads:** 4 or 6
- **Target length:** 20
- **Sound:** Web Audio

## What you can do

1. **Growing color sequence**
  Each round repeats the previous sequence and adds one new colored pad.
2. **Four or six pads**
  Classic four-pad mode or expert six-pad mode for a bigger challenge.
3. **Tones for every pad**
  Each pad plays a distinct note, turning the sequence into a melody.
4. **Sound and mute controls**
  Toggle audio at any time with the button or M key.
5. **Round and score tracking**

## How it works

Simon is a classic electronic memory game. A ring of colored pads lights up and plays a tone one after another, forming a sequence. Your job is to repeat that sequence by tapping the pads in the same order. Every round the game repeats the whole sequence and adds one new pad to the end, so the pattern grows longer and the pressure mounts. A single wrong tap ends the round.

## How to Play

1. **Press Start** — the first pad lights up and plays its tone
2. **Watch the whole sequence** — let every pad flash before you move
3. **Repeat it in order** — tap the pads in the exact same sequence
4. **Each round adds one** — the sequence grows by one pad every round
5. **One mistake ends it** — there are no second chances within a round
6. **Track your progress** — round and score update as you go
7. **Mute if needed** — toggle sound with the Mute button or M key
8. **Replay** — after a game over, press Replay or R to start again

## Rules

- **Growing sequence**: Each round repeats the previous sequence and appends one new random pad.
- **Exact order**: You must tap the pads in the same order they flashed, including repeats.
- **Immediate feedback**: Each pad flashes and plays its tone when you tap it.
- **Game over**: A single wrong pad, or tapping out of order, ends the round immediately.
- **Winning**: Reach the target sequence length (20 pads) to win a round.
- **Scoring**: Score increases by the length of each completed sequence.
- **Best scores**: Your best score and best round are saved locally in your browser.

## Tips and Strategies

- **Turn colors into sound**: Each pad has a fixed tone. Humming the melody is often easier than remembering colors.
- **Chunk the sequence**: Break long runs into groups of three or four, like a phone number.
- **Rehearse only the tail**: Since each round is last round plus one, focus on locking in the newest pad.
- **Do not rush**: The game waits for you. Slow, even taps beat hurried ones.
- **Watch the whole flash**: Do not reach for the first pad until the sequence ends.
- **Use labels**: Give each pad a name or number and say it aloud as it flashes.

## History

Simon was launched by Milton Bradley in 1978 and became one of the defining electronic toys of its era. Its ring of four colored pads, each with its own musical note, made it instantly recognizable. The game was designed by Ralph Baer and Howard Morrison, inspired by an Atari arcade game called Touch Me, but Simon’s clean design and catchy tones turned it into a mainstream hit.

Named after the children’s game Simon Says, it has stayed in production for decades and remains a common example of an easy-to-learn game that quickly becomes fiendishly hard.
