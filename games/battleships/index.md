# Battleships

The classic fleet-hunting game, no-touch edition. Place five ships with a cell of water between each, trade shots with a probability-hunting computer opponent, and sink all seventeen enemy cells.

## At a glance

- **Grids:** 2 × 10×10
- **Fleet:** 5 ships, 17 cells
- **AI strategy:** Probability hunt/target
- **Offline support:** Yes

## What you can do

1. **No-touch fleet placement**
  Five ships (17 cells) on two 10×10 grids. Ships never overlap and never touch: one clear cell of water between every pair, diagonals included.
2. **An editor that shows its verdicts**
  A live preview marks a legal cell green and an illegal one red with the reason, using the same validator the game commits through.
3. **A probability-hunting computer**
  The opponent weighs every possible position of the ships still afloat, honours the gap rule, and treats the water around a sunk ship as known empty sea.
4. **Legitimate information only**
  The AI reads nothing but its own shot results: it cannot see your fleet, repeats no cell, and its tie-breaks are seeded and reproducible.
5. **Move, undo, or reroll**
  Tap a set ship to move it, Escape puts a lifted ship back, Undo takes back the last placement, and Random builds a complete legal fleet atomically.
6. **Works offline**
  Runs entirely in your browser with offline service worker caching.

## How it works

Hide a fleet of five ships on a 10×10 grid, then hunt the computer’s fleet across the water. Every shot is answered: hits are marked, sunk ships are announced, and the first side to find all seventeen enemy cells wins. No account or network connection is required once the game resources are cached.

## Rules in One Breath

Each side commands a Carrier (5 cells), Battleship (4), Cruiser (3), Submarine (3) and Destroyer (2). Ships lie horizontally or vertically, never overlap, and never touch: at least one completely empty cell of water separates every two ships, diagonals included. Players alternate, one shot per turn. A hit is marked with a cross, a miss with a dot, and when the last cell of a ship takes a hit, the ship sinks and is announced. The first fleet to the deep loses.

## Placing Your Fleet

Select a ship in the fleet list, choose its orientation (or press R), and tap a cell. The live preview runs the same validator the game commits through: a legal position shows green, an illegal one shows red and names the reason, whether it would run off the board, overlap a hull, or touch another ship. A tap always places exactly the ship shown as selected, and only a legal position is ever committed. After a placement the selection clears, so the next ship is always your own choice. Tap a set ship in the list to move it, press Escape to return a lifted ship to its original spot, take back a placement with Undo, or let Random lay down a complete legal fleet. The battle cannot begin until all five ships pass the engine’s full fleet check.

## The Computer Opponent

The computer plays a hunt-and-target strategy under the same no-touch rules and with the same information you have: it sees only the results of its own shots.

- **Hunting**, it weights every cell by how many positions of the remaining ships could still cover it, then fires at the strongest cell, preferring the checkerboard parity of the smallest ship afloat. Its search knows the gap rule: placements that would touch a wounded ship are impossible, and the water around a sunk ship is known to be empty without firing a shot there.
- **Targeting**, after a hit it works the neighbouring cells, follows the line once two hits line up, and returns to hunting when the ship sinks. Two hits side by side always belong to one hull, because ships never touch.

Its fleet is generated by the same randomized backtracking and full-fleet validation as yours. It never reads your board, never shoots the same cell twice, and its tie-breaks come from a seeded generator, so a match can be replayed exactly.

## Playing by Hand

Restart replays the same two fleets; New Game lays fresh water. Your boards stay readable at every size, the two grids stack on a phone, and every shot is described in words as well as marks. A dedicated service worker caches the game for offline play, and nothing about your games ever leaves the device.
