# People of Hoi An

A photographic study of people, rituals and everyday life in Hoi An.

- **Category:** PHOTOGRAPHY
- **Year:** 2026
- **Location:** Hoi An, Vietnam
- **Format:** Photographic project

![Hoi An — an evening along the river](https://ik.imagekit.io/awilum/photography/people-of-hoian/hoi-an-2.jpg)

**A photographic journey into the quiet moments, and everyday life of one of Vietnam’s most atmospheric towns.** I began photographing Hội An while based in **Da Nang in 2025**. Just a short motorbike ride away, the town quickly became a place I returned to again and again — not simply to photograph its famous streets, but to **observe the people who make them alive**. I would arrive without a fixed destination, walk through the old streets, local neighborhoods, markets, and quieter corners, and simply see what was happening around me.

![Portrait in Hoi An](https://ik.imagekit.io/awilum/photography/people-of-hoian/sergey-romanenko.jpg)

## Beyond the Lanterns

Hội An is often represented through its **yellow walls, lanterns, ancient houses, and reflections on the river**. But there is another Hội An. It is the woman sitting quietly outside her home. The person working in the street. The elderly resident watching the world pass by. The young generation absorbed in a phone. The conversations, pauses, gestures, and fleeting expressions that rarely become part of the official image of the city.

![Portrait in Hoi An](https://ik.imagekit.io/awilum/photography/people-of-hoian/hoi-an-1.jpg)

**This project is about that Hội An.** Rather than photographing the town as a postcard, I wanted to understand it through the people who live and work there every day.

## The People

The idea behind *People of Hội An* was simple: **to capture the soul of a place through its people.** I was interested in ordinary moments — people working, resting, waiting, talking, or simply existing within their surroundings. These were not staged scenes created for the camera. They were **fragments of real life**, discovered while walking through the city. As a foreigner carrying a camera, I was often welcomed with something very small: a glance, a smile, a moment of curiosity, or a brief acknowledgment. For me, these encounters became just as important as the photographs themselves. **Photography became a way of creating a connection between two strangers.**

![Portrait in Hoi An](https://ik.imagekit.io/awilum/photography/people-of-hoian/hoi-an-1.jpg)

## Portraits Without Performance

The portraits are intentionally intimate and largely unposed. I wanted the viewer to feel close to the people in the photographs — to see their expressions, their eyes, their posture, and the environment surrounding them. **I was not looking for perfect portraits. I was looking for honest ones.** A face can reveal something that words cannot. A gesture can tell a story. A moment of silence can sometimes say more than an entire conversation. This is why the project focuses on **presence rather than performance**.

## Why Black and White?

I chose **black and white photography** as the primary visual language of the project. Hội An is full of color, but color can sometimes become the subject itself. By removing it, I could bring greater attention to **light, texture, emotion, faces, gestures, and human presence**. The old walls, wrinkles, shadows, clothing, streets, and expressions become part of the same visual language. Monochrome also creates a sense of timelessness — connecting different generations and allowing contemporary moments to exist alongside the older character of the town.

## A Town That Moves Slowly

One of the things that stayed with me most strongly was **the rhythm of life in Hội An**. Compared with larger Vietnamese cities such as Ho Chi Minh City, Hội An feels slower and more reflective. There is less urgency. People seem to have more time to simply exist within their surroundings. For a street photographer, this creates a unique environment. Instead of waiting for dramatic events, I began looking for **small gestures, quiet expressions, passing encounters, and ordinary moments that might otherwise disappear**. The slower rhythm of Hội An encourages you to stop. To look. To wait. And sometimes, to simply watch.

![Portrait in Hoi An](https://ik.imagekit.io/awilum/photography/people-of-hoian/hoi-an-2.jpg)

## The City as a Character

The people are at the heart of the project, but Hội An itself is never just a background. **The environment becomes part of every story.** Old walls. Faded paint. Narrow streets. Yellow houses. River reflections. Evening light. Plastic chairs. Market stalls. These details create the world in which the people of Hội An live. The architecture gives the photographs their atmosphere, but **the people give the place its soul**.

## More Than a Collection of Portraits

*People of Hội An* began as a way of documenting the people I encountered during my regular journeys from Da Nang. Over time, it became something more personal. It became an attempt to **preserve a particular period of life** — the streets I walked, the people I met, the conversations I had, and the quiet moments that might otherwise have disappeared. The project is also a record of how I was learning to look at the world. Every photograph became a small act of attention. A decision to stop. To notice. To connect. And to remember.

## The Project

**People of Hội An is not an attempt to create a perfect portrait of the city.** It is a collection of encounters. A visual diary of people and everyday life. A study of the relationship between **people, place, time, and observation**. Most importantly, it is a reminder that the character of a city is not found only in its landmarks. **It is found in the people who wake up there, work there, wait there, grow old there, and quietly make the city what it is.**
