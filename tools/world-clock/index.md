# The World Clock

A live clock for every city that matters to you — the current time, date, and daylight for your city and anywhere your people are, all at once.

## At a glance

- **Cities on wall:** Up to 12
- **Time formats:** 2
- **Quick sets:** 8
- **Offline support:** Yes

## What you can do

1. **See current time everywhere**
  Live clocks for every city on your wall, updating every second.
2. **Know the offset**
  See how far ahead or behind each city is in plain words (e.g., "5 hours ahead").
3. **Day and night at a glance**
  Each city shows day/night status with sunrise and sunset times.
4. **Quick city sets**
  Add one of the 8 preset groups — Europe, The Americas, Digital Nomad and more — as a whole wall.
5. **Works offline**
  Runs entirely in your browser using the IANA time zone database — no server needed.

## How it works

The World Clock for travellers

It is 9 in the morning where you are and time to call the office in London — or the family in Manila, or the photographer in Reykjavík. This clock shows you the current time everywhere at once, so you never have to work out the offset in your head again.

The tool detects your own time zone from your browser and pins it to the nearest city from the catalogue, marking that row with a “You” badge so it can never be mistaken for somewhere remote. When the browser cannot name a matching city, the row shows the time zone itself rather than guessing.

Every other city you add shows the time right now, its local date, how far ahead or behind you it is in words, and whether it is day or night there — with the day of the week spelled out, so a call planned “for Tuesday” lands on the right Tuesday. A city a calendar day ahead or behind says so outright: Yesterday, Today or Tomorrow.

Day and night, and the sunrise and sunset times under each city, are calculated on your device from the city’s coordinates. Nothing is fetched while you use it: no time server, no sunrise table, no network request that could fail on a slow connection or in a place with no signal. The clock keeps ticking with the page open, and pauses only while the tab is in the background.

Times are written in AM/PM by default and you can switch to 24-hour; your choice is remembered. Offsets are never hard-coded — they come from the IANA time zone database for the moment you are looking at, so daylight saving, half-hour and quarter-hour zones such as India and Nepal are always right.

The World Clock shares its city catalogue, time zones and presets with the Time Difference Calculator, so the two tools agree about everywhere on earth. It is served from this website, needs no installation, and works fully offline once the page is open.
